CHECK-UP & DIAGNOSIS

Before we treat, we understand.

A good dental visit starts with knowing what is really happening.

A dental check-up helps us look beyond a single tooth or symptom, understand your overall oral health and identify problems early.

What happens at a check-up ↓ Common Questions

IN SIMPLE WORDS

What happens during a dental check-up?

A check-up is more than simply looking for cavities. We look at your teeth, gums, bite and existing dental work, and listen to anything that has been bothering you.

If something needs a closer look, we explain what we found and discuss whether any further examination is useful.

The goal is simple: understand your dental health before deciding on treatment.

🔎

You don't have to wait for pain to see a dentist.

Regular check-ups can help identify dental problems while they are still easier to manage.

PULP DIAGNOSTIC TESTING

Three simple tests.
Better diagnosis.

We use controlled tests to understand how a tooth responds and to help determine the condition of the pulp before planning treatment.

01
THERMAL SENSIBILITY

Cold Test

A controlled cold stimulus is applied to the tooth and the response is compared with nearby teeth. This helps us understand whether the pulp's sensory nerves respond and whether the response appears normal or prolonged.

✓ Helps assess pulpal response
02
THERMAL SENSIBILITY

Heat Test

A controlled heat stimulus may be used when additional information is needed, especially when warmth or hot foods are associated with the patient's symptoms.

✓ Helps investigate heat-related symptoms
03
PULP VITALITY

Pulp Vitality Test

True vitality assessment looks at the blood supply within the pulp. In clinical practice, vitality and sensibility findings are interpreted together with symptoms, examination and dental X-rays.

✓ Helps assess whether pulp tissue is vital

WHAT DO WE CHECK?

We look at your mouth as a whole.

Your symptoms are important, but so is the bigger picture. A routine assessment can include several parts of your oral health.

01

Teeth

Looking for decay, cracks, wear, sensitivity and other changes.

02

Gums

Checking for signs of inflammation, bleeding or gum problems.

03

Bite & jaw

Looking at how your teeth meet and whether there are signs of excessive wear or other concerns.

04

Existing dental work

Checking fillings, crowns and other restorations for signs of wear or damage.

05

Symptoms

Understanding pain, sensitivity, swelling or anything else you have noticed.

06

Overall oral health

Bringing the findings together rather than looking at one tooth in isolation.

WHEN SHOULD YOU COME?

You don't need to know what's wrong before you book.

If something feels different, looks unusual or simply doesn't feel right, a check-up is a good place to start.

01

Something hurts

Toothache, pain while biting or discomfort that keeps returning.

02

Something is sensitive

Hot, cold or sweet foods cause unusual or lingering sensitivity.

03

Something looks different

A dark spot, crack, swelling, chipped tooth or other change.

04

It has simply been a while

You want to know whether everything is healthy and on track.

WHEN MORE INFORMATION IS NEEDED

Sometimes we need to look a little closer.

Not every patient needs additional tests. If something needs further assessment, we explain why before recommending it.

01

Dental X-rays

X-rays can help us see areas that cannot be fully assessed during a visual examination.

02

Digital photographs

Photographs can help document findings and make it easier to explain what we see.

03

Detailed assessment

Some concerns may need a more focused examination before a treatment decision is made.

04

Specialist opinion

When appropriate, we may discuss whether another dental specialist should be involved.

PREVENTION & LONG-TERM CARE

Keep your teeth healthy for longer.

Good dental care is not only about treating problems after they appear. Prevention focuses on maintaining oral health, identifying changes early and addressing small problems before they become more complicated.

With regular examinations, professional cleaning, personalised oral-hygiene guidance and appropriate follow-up, the aim is to help you maintain your natural teeth and oral health over the long term.

THE PREVENTIVE APPROACH

Prevention is about protecting what is already healthy.

Preventive dentistry aims to reduce the risk of dental problems and identify developing issues before they require more extensive treatment.

Dental health is an ongoing process.

Teeth and gums change over time. Oral hygiene, diet, existing restorations, habits, age and other factors can all influence dental health.

Regular dental care provides an opportunity to monitor these changes rather than waiting until pain or a visible problem appears.

The purpose of preventive care is therefore not to recommend treatment unnecessarily. It is to understand the current condition of your mouth, identify genuine risks and take appropriate steps to maintain health.

01

Prevent

Reduce avoidable risks through good oral hygiene, professional care and healthy daily habits.

02

Detect Early

Regular examinations can help identify changes before they develop into larger dental problems.

03

Maintain

Continued care helps maintain treated and untreated teeth over the long term.

WHY PREVENTION MATTERS

Prevention can make dental care simpler over time.

Many dental problems develop gradually. Regular care can help identify them while they are still limited.

01

Identify Problems Early

Dental decay, gum problems, worn restorations and other changes may not always cause symptoms in their early stages.

Regular examinations provide an opportunity to identify changes before they become painful or require more extensive treatment.

02

Protect Natural Teeth

Preventive care supports the long-term health of your natural teeth and gums.

Maintaining good oral hygiene and addressing problems appropriately can help reduce the risk of avoidable damage to teeth.

03

Avoid Unnecessary Complexity

When a problem is identified early, the treatment required may sometimes be simpler than it would be after the problem has progressed.

Prevention therefore forms an important part of a long-term approach to dental care.

THE BUILDING BLOCKS

What does preventive dental care involve?

Prevention is not one procedure. It is a combination of regular assessment, professional care and habits that support oral health between appointments.

01

Regular Dental Examinations

Regular examinations allow the condition of the teeth, gums and existing restorations to be monitored over time.

They can also provide an opportunity to identify changes that may not yet be causing noticeable symptoms.

02

Professional Cleaning

Professional cleaning can help remove plaque and deposits that may be difficult to remove completely through routine brushing alone.

The frequency of professional cleaning depends on individual oral-health needs rather than a single schedule that applies to everyone.

03

Oral Hygiene Guidance

Effective prevention depends heavily on what happens at home every day.

Personalised guidance can help patients understand how to clean their teeth and gums effectively and where their current routine may need improvement.

04

Early Detection

Detecting changes early can help determine whether an area needs treatment, preventive attention or simply monitoring.

The aim is to respond appropriately rather than waiting until a dental problem becomes more advanced.

EVERYDAY ORAL CARE

What you do between dental visits matters too.

Professional dental care works together with daily habits. Small, consistent steps can have an important role in maintaining oral health.

01

Brush Regularly

Regular toothbrushing helps remove plaque from the tooth surfaces and supports healthy gums.

The technique, duration and consistency of brushing are important, not simply the act of brushing itself.

02

Clean Between Teeth

Toothbrush bristles cannot effectively reach every surface between teeth.

Appropriate interdental cleaning can help remove plaque from these areas and support gum health.

03

Be Mindful of Your Diet

The frequency of exposure to sugars and other dietary factors can influence the risk of dental decay.

Understanding eating and drinking habits can therefore be an important part of preventive advice.

04

Don't Wait for Pain

Pain is not always the first sign that something has changed in the mouth.

Regular assessment can sometimes identify a problem before it becomes uncomfortable or disruptive.

INDIVIDUAL RISK

Preventive care should be personalised.

Everyone does not have the same dental risks. Preventive care should take your individual oral health and circumstances into account.

01

Previous Dental Problems

A history of cavities, gum problems or repeated dental treatment may influence the level of preventive attention that is appropriate.

02

Existing Restorations

Fillings, crowns and other restorations can change over time. Monitoring them can help identify wear, damage or other changes.

03

Oral Hygiene

Individual brushing and interdental cleaning habits can influence plaque accumulation and gum health.

04

Lifestyle & Habits

Diet, tobacco use, oral habits and other lifestyle factors can influence oral health and may be discussed during preventive care.

LONG-TERM CARE

Dental care does not end when treatment is completed.

Maintaining the results of treatment and protecting your remaining natural teeth requires continued attention.

01

Review

Follow-up appointments provide an opportunity to assess treated teeth, existing restorations and overall oral health.

02

Monitor

Not every finding requires immediate treatment. In appropriate situations, monitoring can help determine whether an area is stable or changing.

03

Maintain

Good daily oral hygiene and professional preventive care help support the health of teeth and gums between appointments.

04

Adapt

Preventive recommendations may change over time as your dental condition, habits and needs change.

THINK LONG TERM

From prevention to maintenance

Good dental care can be viewed as a continuous cycle rather than a series of isolated appointments.

01

Assess

Understand the current condition of your teeth and gums and identify areas that may need attention.

02

Prevent

Address risk factors and establish habits that support good oral health.

03

Treat When Needed

If a genuine dental problem is identified, treat it appropriately rather than allowing it to progress.

04

Maintain

Continue regular care and monitoring to protect the health achieved through treatment and prevention.

OUR APPROACH

Prevention should be thoughtful, not automatic.

Preventive dentistry is about understanding your individual needs, identifying genuine risks and recommending care that has a clear purpose.

The goal is to help you maintain your natural teeth, recognise problems early and avoid treatment that may not be necessary.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Prevention & Long-Term Care — FAQs

Some common questions about maintaining dental health over the long term.

Some dental problems can develop without obvious symptoms in their early stages. Regular examinations provide an opportunity to monitor the condition of your teeth and gums and identify changes before they become more significant.

There is no single interval that is appropriate for everyone. The frequency of examinations can depend on your oral-health condition, previous dental problems, existing restorations and individual risk factors.

Daily brushing is essential, but some plaque and deposits can remain in areas that are difficult to clean completely at home. Professional cleaning may be recommended depending on your individual oral-health needs.

The purpose of preventive dentistry is not to increase treatment. It is to maintain oral health, identify genuine problems early and avoid unnecessary progression of dental disease wherever possible.

Consistent oral hygiene, cleaning between teeth, being mindful of dietary habits and attending appropriate dental reviews all contribute to long-term oral health. Your individual routine can be adjusted according to your dental needs.

LOOK AFTER YOUR SMILE FOR THE LONG TERM

Prevention today can make tomorrow's dental care simpler.

Regular assessment and personalised preventive care can help you understand your oral health, identify problems early and maintain your natural teeth over time.

## Platform decision

**Recommendation (pending final confirmation):** move off Wix. Build a **custom-coded website** (HTML/CSS/JS, fully mobile-responsive), hosted on **Vercel or Netlify** with a connected custom domain.

Why: I can edit this directly and instantly whenever asked — no drag-and-drop tool in between. It's faster, more unique visually (not a Wix template), and performs better on mobile/SEO. Cost is just the domain (~₹800–1500/yr); hosting is free at this scale.

Trade-off to flag: Vishesh/his wife won't be able to casually drag-and-drop edit it themselves later — changes go through me (or a developer reading the code). Given Vishesh wants me directly involved long-term as marketing lead anyway, this fits.

## Website plan — structure

1. **Home** — immediate impression: name, one-line positioning ("Precision restorative dentistry, done right"), hero CTA (Book Appointment / WhatsApp), trust signals above the fold
2. **About the Doctor** — credentials, philosophy (ethical, patient-first, no overselling), photo
3. **Why Us / The Magnification Difference** — a dedicated section making the high-magnification, composite-restoration expertise tangible (this is the "catchy, unique" hook — most local clinic sites don't have a real differentiator like this)
4. **Services** — general + restorative dentistry breakdown
5. **Smile Gallery / Before-After** (if photos available)
6. **Testimonials / Reviews**
7. **Location & Contact** — map, address, phone, WhatsApp click-to-chat, hours
8. **Book Appointment** — form or WhatsApp/call CTA repeated

Design direction: clean, clinical-but-warm (not sterile), color palette to be confirmed against the existing Wix draft or built fresh, generous whitespace, mobile-first.

## Photo guidelines (based on first batch received, 11 Aug)

First batch (4 photos) reviewed: 3 operatory/interior shots were sideways (phone rotation not saved) and shot too close/wide, catching cables, papers, and mirror clutter — need retakes, not just crops. One shot (Dr. Gupta in magnification loupes, mid-procedure, patient in chair) is exactly right and should be the model for future clinical shots.

**Keep shooting like this:** candid, in-motion, camera at a slight side/three-quarter angle to doctor's face, loupes/magnification clearly visible, natural light or the operatory light on, patient present but face out of frame or blurred for privacy.

**For interior/clinic shots, retake with:** landscape orientation (double check phone rotation before sending), shooter standing back further (avoid fisheye/too-close distortion), counters tidied first (no loose papers, water bottles, personal items in frame), shot from doorway or room corner at chest height, lights fully on.

**Still want:** reception/waiting area, exterior signage/entrance (helps "centrally located" claim), 2-3 more candid clinical shots from different angles/procedures, a tidied full-room establishing shot.

**Review count:** on the trust strip, show "5.0★ Rated on Google" without stating the review count (currently 10) — avoids it reading as sparse.

IN SIMPLE WORDS

What is a tooth extraction?

Tooth extraction is the removal of a tooth from its socket when keeping the tooth is no longer suitable or predictable.

Before recommending extraction, the condition of the tooth and surrounding tissues is assessed carefully. Whenever possible, treatment options for preserving the tooth are considered first.

The aim is simple: remove the tooth when clinically necessary while keeping the treatment as comfortable and carefully planned as possible.

🦷

Extraction is not always the first option.

Depending on the condition of the tooth, restorative or other treatment options may sometimes be considered.

WHEN MAY EXTRACTION BE NEEDED?

Every extraction begins with understanding the tooth.

The need for extraction depends on the condition of the tooth, surrounding tissues and the available treatment options.

01

Severely damaged tooth

A tooth with extensive damage may sometimes not be predictably restorable.

02

Advanced decay

Extensive decay can weaken a tooth to a point where removal may need to be considered.

03

Problematic wisdom teeth

Some wisdom teeth may require removal when they are causing specific dental problems.

04

Infection or inflammation

In certain situations, removal may form part of the treatment plan after appropriate assessment.

05

Retained roots

Remaining root fragments may require assessment and removal when clinically indicated.

06

Planned dental treatment

In selected cases, extraction may be part of a wider treatment plan for restoring dental health.

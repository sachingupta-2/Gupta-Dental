EMERGENCY DENTAL CARE

When dental problems can't wait.

Sudden tooth pain, swelling, a broken tooth or a dental injury can be distressing. Our approach to urgent dental care begins with understanding the problem, relieving discomfort where possible and deciding what treatment should come next.

URGENT DENTAL PROBLEMS

Not every dental problem should be put off.

Dental problems can sometimes develop suddenly or become uncomfortable without warning. Prompt assessment can help identify the cause of the problem and determine the most appropriate next step.

01

Understand the Problem

A careful examination helps identify the likely source of pain, swelling, damage or other dental symptoms.

02

Address Discomfort

Where appropriate, treatment focuses on addressing the immediate dental problem and helping restore comfort.

03

Plan What Comes Next

Once the urgent concern has been assessed, further treatment can be planned according to the underlying dental condition.

COMMON DENTAL EMERGENCIES

If something feels wrong, it's worth getting checked.

Urgent dental care may be appropriate for a range of situations. The seriousness of a problem cannot always be determined by symptoms alone, so an examination may be recommended.

01

Severe or Sudden Toothache

Persistent, severe or suddenly developing tooth pain may have several causes, including decay, inflammation or problems affecting the tooth or surrounding tissues.

02

Dental Swelling

Swelling around a tooth, gum or face can require prompt assessment to determine its cause and the appropriate course of treatment.

03

Broken or Chipped Tooth

A damaged tooth may require timely assessment, particularly when there is pain, sensitivity or significant loss of tooth structure.

04

Lost Filling or Crown

A lost or damaged restoration can expose the underlying tooth and may require assessment and appropriate restorative care.

05

Dental Injury

An injury affecting a tooth or surrounding structures should be assessed promptly, particularly if the tooth has been displaced, fractured or knocked out.

06

Persistent Dental Sensitivity

New or significant sensitivity, especially when it persists or is associated with pain, may require clinical assessment.

OUR APPROACH

First understand the cause, then treat the problem.

Emergency dental care is not simply about treating the symptom. We first try to understand what is causing the problem and assess the condition of the affected tooth and surrounding tissues.

Depending on the diagnosis, treatment may focus on relieving the immediate problem, protecting the tooth or planning definitive treatment.

✓
Clinical examination

We assess the affected area and understand your symptoms and dental history.

✓
Diagnosis

The findings help determine the likely source of the dental problem.

✓
Immediate management

Where appropriate, treatment is directed towards managing the urgent dental concern.

✓
Definitive treatment planning

Further treatment is discussed once the underlying problem has been understood.

TREATMENT MAY VARY

The right treatment depends on the cause.

Once the dental problem has been assessed, the appropriate treatment depends on what is causing the symptoms and the condition of the affected tooth.

01

Restorative Treatment

Damaged or decayed teeth may require appropriate restorative treatment to rebuild and protect the remaining tooth structure.

02

Root Canal Treatment

When the dental pulp is affected, root canal treatment may be considered where the tooth can be predictably saved and maintained.

03

Dental Extraction

In some situations, a tooth may not be predictably restorable and extraction may need to be considered.

04

Management of Dental Injury

Injured teeth require assessment to determine the extent of damage and whether the tooth can be protected or restored.

WHAT TO EXPECT

A calm and structured approach to urgent care.

Dental emergencies can feel stressful. Our aim is to keep the process clear, focused and centred on understanding your immediate concern.

01

Listen

We understand what happened, when the symptoms began and what you are experiencing.

02

Examine

The affected tooth and surrounding structures are carefully examined.

03

Stabilise

Where appropriate, the immediate dental concern is managed to restore comfort and protect the area.

04

Restore

Further treatment is planned according to the diagnosis and the long-term needs of the tooth.

WHEN TO SEEK URGENT HELP

Don't wait if symptoms are getting worse.

Severe or worsening dental pain, significant swelling, dental trauma or other rapidly developing symptoms should be assessed promptly.

If there is significant facial swelling affecting breathing or swallowing, uncontrolled bleeding, or a serious injury, seek emergency medical care immediately.

01

Severe or rapidly worsening pain

02

Significant swelling of the face or mouth

03

Significant dental injury or trauma

04

Difficulty breathing or swallowing

NEED URGENT DENTAL CARE?

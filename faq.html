Getting started & preventive care

Examinations, prevention and making informed choices.

Your first visit generally begins with a conversation about your concerns, dental history and what you would like help with. The dentist will examine your teeth and gums and may recommend additional investigations if they are needed to understand the problem.

The findings and suitable options are discussed with you before treatment is planned, so you can ask questions and make an informed decision.

The recommended review interval depends on your dental health, history and individual risk factors.

There is no single interval that suits everyone. Your dentist can suggest a review schedule based on your oral health, previous dental problems, existing restorations and risk factors.

Brush twice daily with fluoride toothpaste, clean between your teeth every day, and limit frequent sugary snacks and drinks. Regular dental reviews can help identify areas that need extra attention.

Your dentist can tailor prevention advice to your teeth, gums, diet and oral-hygiene routine.

02

Appointments & visit planning

What to prepare and how to plan your visit.

You can contact the clinic by phone or WhatsApp to request a suitable appointment time. You can also use the Book Appointment option on the website.

Call +91 99908 80975 or message WhatsApp to get in touch.

Bring any relevant previous dental records or X-rays you have, along with a list of medicines you take and any known allergies or important medical conditions. Please mention previous dental treatment and any changes in your health.

Appointment length depends on the reason for your visit and whether an examination, diagnostic imaging or treatment is needed. When booking, explain your concern so the clinic can advise you about the time to allow.

Please contact the clinic as early as possible if you need to change your appointment. The team can help you discuss another suitable time.

03

Treatments & procedures

Understand common treatments and why recommendations vary.

Root canal treatment is carried out using appropriate local anaesthesia to help keep you comfortable during the procedure. The experience varies with the condition of the tooth and the nature of the infection or inflammation.

Some tenderness may occur afterward. The dentist will explain what to expect and how to manage it.

A filling may be recommended when decay or a localised defect has damaged part of a tooth and the remaining structure can be restored this way.

The appropriate option depends on the size, depth and condition of the tooth. The aim is to address the problem while preserving sound tooth structure wherever possible.

No. A damaged tooth does not automatically need to be removed. Depending on the findings, options may include a filling, other restoration, root canal treatment or a protective crown.

The decision depends on the extent of damage, the tooth's restorability, surrounding tissues and your overall dental needs.

Plaque can harden into deposits that are difficult to remove with brushing alone. Professional cleaning helps remove these deposits and can be combined with advice on improving everyday oral hygiene.

The type and frequency of cleaning recommended depend on your gum health and the amount of deposit present.

Where clinically appropriate, preserving natural tooth structure is an important consideration. Treatment is planned after examination and diagnosis, with the aim of treating the problem while avoiding unnecessary removal of healthy tooth structure.

Sometimes a tooth may be too damaged or have a poor outlook; the reasons and alternatives should be discussed with you.

A rubber dam is a thin sheet used to isolate a tooth from the rest of the mouth during certain procedures, particularly many root canal treatments. It helps keep the working area dry and reduces contamination from saliva.

Your dentist will decide when it is appropriate for the procedure being performed.

Magnification helps the dentist see small details more clearly during selected procedures. It can support careful assessment and controlled work, particularly when treating fine tooth structures.

It is an aid to clinical judgement, not a guarantee of a particular treatment outcome.

An examination is needed to discuss which options are suitable for your situation.

04

Recovery & aftercare

Practical guidance after treatment and when to seek advice.

If local anaesthesia was used, avoid chewing until the numbness has worn off so you do not accidentally bite your cheek, lip or tongue. Follow the specific instructions given by your dentist, as advice can vary with the procedure and material used.

Some tenderness around the treated tooth can occur after treatment and should gradually improve. Follow the aftercare instructions provided and attend any recommended review or restorative appointment.

Contact the clinic if pain is severe, symptoms worsen, swelling develops or you are concerned about your recovery.

Sensitivity can have several causes, including exposed dentine, gum recession, decay, a crack or a recent dental procedure. The significance depends on the pattern, duration and severity of the symptoms.

Arrange an assessment if sensitivity persists, worsens, occurs spontaneously or affects your ability to eat or sleep.

Antibiotics are not needed for every toothache and do not replace treatment of the underlying dental cause. They should only be taken when prescribed by an appropriate clinician.

Seek dental assessment for the cause of the pain, particularly if symptoms are worsening or there is swelling or fever.

A restoration that feels too high or makes your bite feel uneven should be reviewed. Contact the clinic rather than trying to adjust it yourself. A dentist can check the bite and decide whether an adjustment is needed.

Follow the instructions given for your specific treatment. Contact the clinic if symptoms are worsening, pain is not settling as expected, swelling develops, your bite feels wrong or you are unsure about your recovery.

Significant facial swelling or difficulty breathing or swallowing requires urgent medical assessment.

05

Urgent dental concerns

Know when a dental concern needs prompt or emergency attention.

Dental pain can have different causes, so an examination is needed to determine the reason. Contact the clinic for advice and assessment, especially if the pain is severe, persistent or worsening.

Do not place aspirin or other medicines directly on the tooth or gum.

Facial or rapidly increasing swelling, particularly with fever or feeling unwell, needs urgent assessment. Difficulty breathing or swallowing is an emergency: seek emergency medical care immediately rather than waiting for a routine appointment.

Contact the clinic promptly and avoid chewing on the damaged tooth. If you find a broken fragment, keep it clean and bring it with you if possible.

The next step depends on the extent of the damage, whether the nerve is affected and any injury to surrounding tissues.

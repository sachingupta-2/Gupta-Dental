CONSERVATIVE DENTISTRY

Preserve more.
Treat only what is needed.

Conservative dentistry is an approach centred around preserving as much of your healthy natural tooth as possible. Instead of removing more tooth structure than necessary, treatment is planned carefully to address the actual problem while protecting the healthy parts of the tooth.

The goal is not simply to repair a tooth today. It is to maintain its strength, function and health for as long as possible.

UNDERSTANDING THE CONCEPT

What is Conservative Dentistry?

Conservative dentistry focuses on treating dental disease while preserving as much healthy natural tooth structure as possible.

Treat the problem without unnecessarily sacrificing the healthy tooth.

A natural tooth is a complex structure designed to function together as a whole. When part of a tooth becomes affected by decay, damage or disease, the treatment should ideally address the affected area while protecting the healthy structure around it.

Conservative dentistry therefore begins with understanding exactly what is happening inside and around the tooth. The treatment is then planned according to the extent of the problem rather than automatically removing more tooth structure than is necessary.

This approach can be particularly important when dealing with early decay, small defects, damaged restorations or teeth where only a limited area requires treatment.

01

Identify the actual problem

Careful examination helps determine whether a tooth genuinely needs treatment and how extensive that treatment needs to be.

02

Preserve healthy structure

When treatment is required, the aim is to avoid unnecessary removal of sound natural tooth structure.

03

Think beyond today's treatment

The condition of the tooth today and how it may respond over time are both considered while planning treatment.

NATURAL TOOTH PRESERVATION

Why preserving your natural tooth matters

Whenever a natural tooth can be predictably maintained, keeping it can be an important part of long-term dental care.

01

Natural Structure

A natural tooth has a structure that has developed to work together as a functional unit. Preserving healthy parts of that structure can help retain the tooth's natural form and function.

Treatment is therefore planned to address the damaged or diseased portion while protecting healthy areas whenever clinically possible.

02

Function & Comfort

Teeth work together during chewing, speaking and everyday activities. Maintaining the natural tooth can help preserve its position and relationship with the surrounding teeth.

The aim is to restore the tooth appropriately rather than treating it in isolation from the rest of the mouth.

03

Long-Term Thinking

Dental treatment should not only solve the immediate problem. It should also consider how the tooth and restoration may need to be maintained in the future.

This is why diagnosis, treatment planning and regular maintenance are important parts of conservative care.

WHEN IS IT USED?

Problems that may benefit from a conservative approach

Conservative dentistry is not a single treatment. It is a way of approaching different dental problems with the aim of preserving healthy tooth structure wherever appropriate.

01

Tooth Decay

Early or limited decay may sometimes be treated by removing only the affected tooth structure and restoring the area with an appropriate filling.

The exact treatment depends on how deep and extensive the decay is and whether the pulp of the tooth is involved.

02

Small Fractures & Defects

Not every damaged or chipped tooth requires extensive treatment. In selected cases, a limited repair may be appropriate.

The location, size and cause of the defect are considered before deciding how the tooth should be restored.

03

Failing Fillings

An old or damaged filling does not always mean that the entire tooth needs extensive treatment.

The tooth is assessed to determine whether the restoration can be repaired, replaced or whether a different treatment is required.

04

Deeper Tooth Problems

When decay or damage has progressed deeper into the tooth, more comprehensive treatment may be necessary.

If the pulp is affected, root canal treatment may be considered when the tooth can still be predictably saved.

OUR TREATMENT PROCESS

Conservative care starts with diagnosis

Treatment is not decided simply by looking at a visible cavity or damaged area. The condition of the tooth is assessed first, followed by a treatment plan based on what is clinically appropriate.

01

Examine

The tooth and surrounding oral structures are carefully examined to understand the condition and identify areas that may require attention.

The examination helps establish a clear starting point before deciding whether treatment is necessary and what level of treatment may be appropriate.

02

Diagnose

The findings are evaluated to determine the nature and extent of the dental problem.

A correct diagnosis is important because similar symptoms can sometimes have different causes. Treatment should be based on the actual condition rather than assumptions.

03

Plan

Treatment options are considered according to the condition of the tooth and the amount of healthy structure remaining.

The plan is individualised according to the tooth, the extent of disease and the patient's overall dental needs.

04

Treat

The selected treatment focuses on addressing the disease or damage while preserving healthy tooth structure wherever clinically appropriate.

Depending on the diagnosis, treatment may involve restoration, repair, replacement of a failing restoration or root canal treatment.

05

Maintain

Once treatment is completed, maintaining the tooth through good oral hygiene, preventive care and appropriate reviews becomes part of long-term care.

Regular maintenance can help monitor treated teeth and identify new problems early.

TREATMENT OPTIONS

What can conservative dentistry involve?

The treatment depends entirely on the condition of the tooth. Conservative dentistry does not mean avoiding necessary treatment. It means choosing appropriate treatment while preserving healthy structure whenever possible.

01

Tooth-Coloured Fillings

When a tooth has a suitable amount of healthy structure remaining, a tooth-coloured restoration may be used to restore an area affected by decay or damage.

The objective is to restore the affected portion while maintaining as much healthy tooth structure as possible.

02

Repair of Damaged Teeth

Selected small chips, defects or damaged areas may sometimes be repaired rather than replacing or removing a larger portion of the tooth.

Whether repair is suitable depends on the size, position and condition of the affected area.

03

Replacement of Failing Restorations

Older restorations may eventually become worn, fractured or compromised. They can be assessed to determine whether replacement is necessary.

The surrounding natural tooth structure is considered when planning the new restoration.

04

Root Canal Treatment

If disease or damage has reached the pulp of a tooth, root canal treatment may sometimes allow the natural tooth to be retained instead of removing it.

The decision depends on whether the tooth can be treated and restored predictably.

05

Preventive Care

Preventing or detecting dental problems early can reduce the amount of treatment a tooth may require later.

Regular examinations, professional cleaning and appropriate oral-hygiene guidance are important parts of this approach.

06

Monitoring & Maintenance

Some dental findings may not require immediate treatment. In suitable situations, monitoring the area can be an appropriate part of the overall plan.

Regular reviews help identify changes early and determine when intervention may actually become necessary.

AN IMPORTANT DISTINCTION

Conservative does not mean avoiding necessary treatment

Preserving natural teeth is important, but preservation should always be balanced with appropriate treatment of dental disease.

01

Not "do nothing"

Conservative dentistry does not mean leaving disease untreated. If a tooth requires treatment, appropriate treatment is recommended.

02

Not every problem needs the same treatment

The extent of treatment should reflect the actual condition of the tooth. A small problem and a severely damaged tooth may require very different approaches.

03

Appropriate treatment comes first

The purpose is to find the most suitable way to manage the dental problem while preserving healthy structure whenever this can be done predictably.

OUR APPROACH

A careful approach to every tooth

Conservative dentistry fits naturally into a broader philosophy of careful diagnosis, appropriate treatment and long-term maintenance.

01

Careful Diagnosis

Understand the problem before deciding how it should be treated. A clear diagnosis helps determine whether treatment is necessary and how extensive it needs to be.

02

Preserve Healthy Structure

Avoid unnecessary removal of healthy natural tooth structure whenever clinically appropriate, while still addressing disease effectively.

03

Appropriate Treatment

Choose treatment according to the actual condition of the tooth rather than applying the same solution to every patient.

04

Long-Term Thinking

Consider how the tooth can be maintained after treatment and how future dental problems may be prevented.
